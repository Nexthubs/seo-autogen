# Brand Visual Guideline

This is the visual identity rulebook for ALL generated article images
(hero and inline). The Image Planner follows it for every prompt.

## Style

- Modern flat-to-soft illustration: clean shapes, gentle gradients,
  subtle grain. NOT photorealistic, NOT 3D render, NOT cartoonish.
- A calm, editorial "wellness magazine" look: sophisticated and
  human, never corporate.
- Line work minimal; forms are rounded and organic.

## Color palette (dominant)

- Warm neutrals: cream, sand, soft taupe (backgrounds).
- One muted accent per image: terracotta, dusty sage, muted teal,
  or soft dusk blue — matched to the article's emotional tone.
- Overall warmth: images should feel like late-afternoon light.

## Mood & tone

- Calm, safe, hopeful, reflective. This is a psychology,
  relationships and personal-growth audience: people recovering from
  hurt, learning about themselves.
- Never: dramatic, dark, frightening, seductive, or clinical (no
  hospital/lab/hospital-diagnosis visuals).

## Subjects & composition

- People are shown from the shoulders up or as silhouettes; faces
  are simplified and unidentifiable (no specific real person).
- Prefer: a single person in a quiet interior or nature scene; two
  people with healthy distance when the article is about a
  relationship; a person with a symbolic object (a window, a door,
  plants, a phone set down, a path).
- Composition: generous negative space around the subject; subject
  placed on the rule-of-thirds; room for overlay text on hero images
  (keep the left third quiet).

## Lighting

- Soft, diffused, warm. Natural window light or golden-hour glow.
- No harsh shadows, no neon, no studio strobe look.

## Negative constraints (always include in prompts)

- No text, no words, no letters, no signage, no logos, no watermarks.
- No photorealistic humans, no medical or clinical settings.
- No crowded scenes, no visible brand names.
